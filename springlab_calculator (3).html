Helical spring experiment.

Read the aim and procedure first, then continue to the lab to perform the experiment.

Aim

01 / OBJECTIVE

To determine the stiffness, deflection and maximum shear stress of a helical compression spring for a given load and spring geometry, and to study how the wire diameter (d), mean coil diameter (D) and number of active coils (n) affect the spring stiffness.

Apparatus

SIMULATION
  • SpringLab virtual simulator (helical compression spring model, SI units)
  • Live spring preview and calculated-results panel
  • Observation table in the Results section

Procedure

STEPS
  1. Read the Theory section to understand the equations used.
  2. Open the Lab section and select Analysis mode.
  3. Enter the applied load F, shear modulus G, wire diameter d, mean coil diameter D and number of active coils n. Optionally enter an allowable shear stress for comparison.
  4. Observe the spring preview and the calculated stiffness k, deflection δ and maximum shear stress τmax.
  5. Click Record reading to save the observation in the Results table.
  6. Change one parameter at a time (d, D or n), keeping the others constant, and record at least 3 readings for each parameter.
  7. Optionally switch to Design / solve mode, enter a target stiffness and solve for d, D or n.
  8. Open the Results section to study the table, and download it if required.
  9. Note your observations in the Conclusion section.

Theory

Equations and assumptions used in the simulator.

Helical compression spring

BACKGROUND

A helical compression spring stores energy by twisting its wire when an axial load is applied. The wire is mainly loaded in torsion, so the stress in the wire is a shear stress.

Spring index

C = D / d

Spring stiffness (rate)

k = G d⁴ / (8 D³ n)

Deflection

δ = F / k

Maximum shear stress

τmax = Kw × (8 F D / π d³)

Wahl correction factor

Kw = (4C − 1)/(4C − 4) + 0.615 / C

Assumptions

  • Close-coiled, round-wire helical compression spring
  • Linear elastic behaviour of the material
  • The Wahl factor accounts for wire curvature and direct shear
  • This is a preliminary calculation, not a complete spring standard or fatigue-life verification

What to look for

ANALYSIS
  • Vary d only: how does k change? (k ∝ d⁴)
  • Vary D only: how does k change? (k ∝ 1/D³)
  • Vary n only: how does k change? (k ∝ 1/n)
  • Compare how τmax changes as d and D change.

Conclusion

Summary of what this experiment shows.

Conclusion

05 / SUMMARY
  • The stiffness of a helical spring increases rapidly with wire diameter (k ∝ d⁴).
  • Increasing the mean coil diameter makes the spring much softer (k ∝ 1/D³).
  • Adding active coils reduces stiffness in proportion (k ∝ 1/n).
  • For a given load, the deflection is δ = F / k, and the maximum shear stress depends mainly on d, D and F (through the Wahl factor Kw).
  • The values obtained are theoretical and valid only under the stated assumptions.
